    Awaiting data GA4

    Same blocker as section 02, plus one export limit worth knowing in advance.

    Google caps each monthly landing page export at roughly 1,000 rows, so page totals run a fraction below site totals once the deepest long tail is cut off. On addus.com that gap is between 0.1% and 0.5%. It is small, it is expected, and it is worth saying out loud before somebody finds it.

Thirteen pages earn any organic ranking at all, and one blog post about a Spanish biochemist takes 55% of the estimated traffic. Ahrefs estimates from its own crawl; Google Analytics will count actual visits. Expect the two to disagree, and quote each with its tool attached.

    The people behind the visits

    Sessions count visits. This section counts people, and credits the channel that first brought them to the site rather than the one that brought them back.

    Awaiting data GA4

    Two counting rules that will apply the moment this section fills.

    Google removes duplicate people, so new users plus returning users will never add up to total users in any month. Adding those two columns together produces a number that does not mean anything, and a returning rate derived from them is wrong.

    The user export also carries no referring domain, which means AI-platform visits cannot be folded in at the person level the way they can at the session level. Those months will read as not reported rather than zero, because they will not be zero. They will be ungrouped.

    What Google showed, and what people clicked

    Search Console reports impressions, clicks, click-through rate and average position straight from Google. It is the cleanest source in the set, because no bot traffic reaches it.

    Awaiting data Search Console

    The property started collecting on 26 August 2026.

    This is the most valuable of the four sources for this pilot, and the most urgent to protect. Search Console keeps only 16 months of history and then deletes the oldest month permanently. Nothing before 26 August 2026 exists for this domain and nothing can recover it, so every month from here needs exporting and storing rather than leaving in the interface.

    Two counting rules to carry when the data arrives. Property totals and page-level sums are different numbers, because one result can serve several pages, so summing the Pages export overstates impressions substantially. And average position is impression-weighted across a date range, not an average of daily averages. On addus.com that distinction moved January's figure from 21.48 to 27.12.
